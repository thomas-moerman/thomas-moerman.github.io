about

Hi, I’m Thomas.

  1. first desktop

    It started with our first desktop at home. I wanted to play games on it. It wanted to crawl. And the internet was so slow it barely counted. I had questions, and nobody in the house had answers.

  2. the it guy

    Help came in the form of a colleague of my father, a man who said he was “an IT guy”. I still remember the long hairs growing out of his ears, and how he tugged at them while he stared at the screen. He clicked “Troubleshoot internet connection” ten times in a row, as if the eleventh try might be the one. It wasn’t. He left without fixing a thing. I was so annoyed that I sat down and started digging myself. I didn’t stop until it worked.

  3. underneath

    Fixing it wasn’t the big deal. What it left behind was. I wanted to know how the digital world actually worked. Not which button to click, but what happens underneath. I still do.

  4. the army

    School was a different story. I was not a good student, and for years I blamed everything around me. The truth was simpler. It was me. That is how I ended up in the military. I liked it for a while, until I no longer felt I belonged, so I left. But I didn’t leave empty-handed. The army gave me a strong ethical compass and a discipline I rely on every day.

  5. msc ai

    Leaving gave me an energy I had never felt before. For the first time, I was a good student. I went on to earn an MSc in Artificial Intelligence, and it brought me straight back to the question that started it all. What happens underneath?

  6. one level up

    I was never the developer who writes the most elegant code, and I don’t try to be. My strength sits one level up. I see how the pieces fit together, and I know how to make AI do things most people still assume it can’t. We have barely scratched the surface. Most people just haven’t felt it yet.

  7. today

    Today I’m the CTO of Forenoon. I want to build great things, keep learning, and help others along the way. Which is exactly what that kid behind our first desktop wanted. Only now, the internet works.
