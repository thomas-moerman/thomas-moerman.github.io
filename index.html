about

Hi, I’m Thomas.

  1. first desktop

    I often wonder how I ended up in tech, and the furthest back I can trace it is the day we got our first desktop at home. It didn’t take long before I started wondering why this PC was so slow at running the games I wanted to play, and why our internet was so bad.

  2. the it guy

    For the second one, help arrived in the form of an older man, a colleague of my father who claimed to be “an IT guy”. I still remember the long hairs growing out of his ears and how he kept pulling at them while he stared at the screen. I watched him click “Troubleshoot internet connection” ten times in a row, as if the eleventh time might finally work. It never did. He left without fixing anything, and it frustrated me so much that I sat down and started digging into it myself, until I fixed it.

  3. underneath

    That moment stuck with me. Fixing our internet wasn’t such a big deal, but it left me with a fascination for how the digital world around me actually worked, and with the feeling that clicking until something works is never enough. I wanted to understand what was happening underneath, and that feeling has never really left.

  4. the army

    School was a different story. I wasn’t a good student when I was young. For a long time I blamed my environment, but at the end of the day it was me, and that is what pushed me towards a job in the military first. I enjoyed it for a while, but at some point I felt I no longer fit in that environment, and I decided to leave. I didn’t leave empty-handed, though. The army gave me a strong ethical compass and a discipline that I still carry with me.

  5. msc ai

    Leaving gave me a renewed energy I hadn’t felt before, and for the first time I turned out to be a good student. I went on to get an MSc in Artificial Intelligence, which was pretty cool, and it brought me right back to that old urge to understand how things work underneath.

  6. honestly

    I’ve never been a great developer, and I’ll be honest about that. What I can do is make AI do great things, and I firmly believe in its potential. I think we have only scratched the surface of what it can do, even if not everyone sees or feels that yet.

  7. today

    Today I’m the CTO of Forenoon, a small startup. I want to build great things, keep learning, and help others along the way. Pretty much what that kid behind our first desktop wanted too, just with better internet now.
